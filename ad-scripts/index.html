01

One of ten PDFs

Pain

Target: salespeople who send "the details" and then get ghosted

Script

A client says, "I'm busy. Just send me the details on WhatsApp." And you say, "Sure, which email?"

The second you send that PDF, you become one of ten. One of ten agents in their WhatsApp. One of ten PDFs they'll never open.

And when you look exactly like the other nine, the only thing left to compare is the price.

I stopped sending details. I started asking for a quick call instead, and I use a method I call The Quiet Yes.

02

Compared to what?

Price

Target: salespeople who lower their price when a client says it's too expensive

Script

Next time a client says, "It's too expensive," don't explain and don't discount.

Ask three words: "Compared to what?" Then shut up. Fully. Let it get awkward.

Nine times out of ten, they don't have an answer. They said it because that's what buyers say.

That's one move of The Quiet Yes, and it's my favourite objection.

03

Stop saying these 5 words

Words

Target: salespeople who sound unsure on calls

Script

Five words that are costing you deals: just, honestly, try, cheap and "does that make sense?"

"Just" makes you sound like you're apologising for existing. "Try" tells the client: maybe not.

It's the same call and the same offer. Only the words are different.

That's the difference between "let me think about it" and "send me the invoice."

04

Dead leads

Revive

Target: salespeople with a long list of leads who left them on read

Script

Dead leads are the easiest money in sales, because they already know you.

Never send "just checking in." Ask what went wrong without a pitch, come back with something new, then send one line: "Are you still looking to do this, or should I close your file?"

A no isn't forever. It's just a no for now.

I call this step Deal CPR. It's one of three moves in The Quiet Yes.

05

How to sell to rich people

Segment

Target: salespeople who sell expensive things in Dubai

Script

Rich clients don't want more. They want less friction.

Stop describing your product. Describe life after it. They can smell desperation, so don't over-explain, don't discount before they ask, and never send "just following up" 17 times.

People with money aren't paying more to get more. A lot of the time, they're paying more to deal with less.
