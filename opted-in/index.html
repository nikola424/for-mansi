D1: Stop Sending “The Details” (Say This Instead)

Preview One of ten PDFs they'll never open.

One of ten PDFs they'll never open.

Hey {FIRST NAME},

"I'm busy right now. Can you just send me the details on WhatsApp?"

If you sell for a living, you heard that sentence this week.

And there's a good chance you answered, "Sure, which email should I send it to?"

Which sounds polite. It's also how you lose the deal.

Because the second you send that PDF, you become one of ten. One of ten agents in their WhatsApp. One of ten PDFs they'll never open.

And when you look exactly like the other nine, the only thing left to compare is the price.

Think about what you just asked them to do. Open your PDF. Read six pages. Compare it with nine other PDFs. Work out which one fits.

That's homework. And nobody does homework for a stranger.

Say this instead:

"I don't want to send you a random package that doesn't fit what you need, and I'm guessing you don't want that either. Why don't we do a quick call tomorrow, around 10 or 11, whatever works, and I'll tell you exactly what makes sense for you? Then I'll send you the details."

If they don't give you 10 minutes, they were never going to give you money.

Try it on the next client who asks for "the details."

D2: Fix Your Hello [COPY ME]

Preview Nobody buys from weak energy.

Nobody buys from weak energy.

Hey {FIRST NAME},

"Hi Mike, is this a good time to talk?"

Almost everyone opens a call like that. And almost every time, Mike says, "Actually, I'm busy right now."

Not because he's busy. Because he didn't get the right feeling from you. You sounded like a robot reading a script, and nobody buys from weak energy.

Here's what I do instead. I call it a curiosity hook:

"Hi Mike, it's Mansi. I see you dropped something on our website this morning. I wanted to understand what you were comparing us against, so I can help you better."

It's the same call, the same company and the same reason for calling. Only the words and the tone are different.

Look at what that opening does:

It interrupts their usual pattern.

It sounds curious, not pushy.

It doesn't look like you're selling. It looks like you're qualifying them.

Swap in your own reason for calling and try it on your next call. Then notice how long Mike stays on the line.

D3: Say “Compared To What?” Then Shut Up

Preview Your new favourite objection.

Your new favourite objection.

Hey {FIRST NAME},

"Honestly, it's too expensive."

Most of us panic. We explain the value again, or we offer a discount.

Do this instead. Ask one question: "Compared to what?"

Then shut up. Fully. Let it get awkward.

Nine times out of ten, they don't have an answer. They said it because that's what buyers do.

And if they do have an answer, it's usually, "Your competitor quoted me something cheaper." Now you know exactly what you're up against.

So you ask the second question:

"Makes sense. Can I ask you something? What are they leaving out of that price? Because a lot of companies take a few things out so the number looks lower."

Then tell them the truth: "I don't do that, because I want a real relationship with you, and I'd like referrals from you later."

Watch their face. They just realised they were comparing a Toyota to a G-Wagon.

You never had to defend your price. You asked two questions, and they did the maths themselves.

That's how I turned "it's too expensive" into my favourite objection.

D4: Dead Leads Are The Easiest Money In Sales

Preview 4 messages that bring them back

4 messages that bring them back

Hey {FIRST NAME},

Dead leads are the easiest money in sales, because they already know you.

Here's how I bring them back, in 4 steps.

1. Ask what went wrong, without a pitch.

"Hey Ahmed, this isn't a pitch. I'm just curious what made you go the other way. It helps me get better."

People love giving feedback. Half the time they say, "It wasn't you, it was just the timing." Now the door is open.

2. Check in when the other company messes up.

"Hey, hope everything's going smoothly with your setup. How are you finding it? If you need anything, I'm just a text away."

That's exactly when the other company disappears, because they already got the payment. And guess who messaged at the right time? You.

3. Come back with something new.

Never "just checking in." Always a reason: "We have a faster option now, and I thought of you straight away." New information is a new conversation.

4. Send the one-line text.

"Hey, are you still looking to [what they wanted], or should I close your file? Either way, please let me know."

It's short, it has no pressure, and it gets more replies than any long message you'll ever write.

A no isn't forever. It's just a no for now.

D5: 5 Words That Make You Sound Unsure

Preview Check your last call. You said at least 3 of these.

Check your last call. You said at least 3 of these.

Hey {FIRST NAME},

Here are five words I want you to stop using on your sales calls. They're a big part of why you're not closing more.

1. "Just." "Just checking in. Just following up. Just let me know." It makes you sound like you're apologising for existing. Say: "Mike, following up on what we discussed yesterday."

2. "Honestly." "Honestly, this is the best price." So everything you said before that was a lie? If it's true, you don't need to announce it.

3. "Try." "I'll try to get it done by Sunday." The client hears: maybe not. Say: "Mike, it'll be done by Sunday." And if you can't promise it, give a date you can.

4. "Cheap" or "affordable." The second you say it, you turned your service into a bargain. Say: "It's an investment of [your price]."

5. "Does that make sense?" Every time I hear an agent say this, I know they're going to lose the deal. Say: "What questions do you have so far?" Now they actually talk.

It's the same call and the same offer, with different words and a different result.

That's the difference between "let me think about it" and "send me the invoice."

D6: What Rich Clients Actually Pay For

Preview It's not more. It's less.

It's not more. It's less.

Hey {FIRST NAME},

I'm writing this on a plane, in first class, so it's the perfect time to explain how to sell to people who have money.

1. Rich clients don't want more. They want less friction. Half the luxury of first class isn't the seat. It's that someone takes my bags, someone handles check-in, and someone asks what I want before I even ask.

2. Don't describe your product. Describe life after it. A normal client asks, "What do I get?" A premium client thinks, "What does this let me do?" Nobody buys first class because they love a bigger chair. They're buying privacy, time and treatment.

3. Premium buyers can smell desperation. Over-explaining, discounting before they've even asked, and sending "just following up" 17 times all put them above you. Whether the deal is small or huge, your energy stays the same.

4. Make the buying experience match the price. The sale starts before they pay. People with money aren't paying more to get more. A lot of the time, they're paying more to deal with less.
