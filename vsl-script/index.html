Pain

Picture the last time a rich client chose another agent over you. On the call, you were polite, you answered every question, and you felt like you had a real connection with them, only to find out a week later that they had bought from someone else.

Or maybe it went the other way. They told you they were busy and asked you to send the details on WhatsApp, so you sent a beautiful PDF within the hour and then waited for a reply that never came, because they had already left you on read.

You worked so hard to get that person on the phone and you walked away with nothing, and the worst part is that you still don't know what you did wrong.

Here's the harsh reality, and I'm saying it with love: it was never your price and it was never your market. It was being nice, because the nicest things you do on a call, like saying yes to the PDF and offering a discount before they even ask, are exactly what makes a rich client lose respect for you and disappear.

I know you have probably tried the usual fixes, more follow-ups, a nicer PDF, a lower price, and none of them worked, because they all make the same mistake of trying harder to be nice. What you want is simple: to sit across from a rich client and hear them say yes at your full price.

In the next ten minutes I'm going to show you what really goes wrong on those calls, and the method I use so rich clients choose me over the other nine agents without me ever lowering my price.

05

Mechanism

The Quiet Yes

Most of us were taught that being helpful and flexible makes a client trust us. With a rich client it works the other way around, because the more you offer, the more they feel they can ignore you, since someone who's easy to get is rarely valued. What they trust is the person who asks the best question, and that's what I learned over years of selling to them.

I turned that into a method I call The Quiet Yes, which is a way of running a sales call so that the client talks themselves into the decision. It has three moves, and I'll show you what each one changes.

Move 1

Fix your hello

This move changes the first thirty seconds of the call. Most people open with, "Is this a good time to talk?" and the client answers, "Actually, I'm busy," because they didn't get the right feeling from you. So you open with curiosity about why they reached out, which makes you sound like you're qualifying them and not selling to them, and nobody buys from weak energy. Inside The Closing Room I give you the exact opening for what you sell.

On screen

"How to start a sales call" · more than 1.1M views

Move 2

The 2 questions

This move changes what happens when a client says, "It's too expensive." You don't explain, you don't defend and you don't discount. You ask two questions in a set order and then you stay silent: the first makes them say what they're really comparing you against, and the second shows them what that cheaper offer leaves out. Most clients work out by themselves that they were comparing a Toyota to a G-Wagon, and you never had to defend your price.

Move 3

Deal CPR: bring dead leads back

Dead leads are the easiest money in sales, because they already know you. Deal CPR is four short messages, sent in a set order, that bring a dead lead back without sounding needy, and the last one is a single line that gets more replies than any long message you'll ever write. A no isn't forever. It's just a no for now.

That's The Quiet Yes. Three moves, and a lot of silence.
